## Star formation in the Milky Way
- Jeans' Mass determines when self-gravitation and "collapse" can occur: $M_J \propto \left(\frac{T}{\mu}\right)^{3/2} \rho^{-1/2}$.[+]
- For a given mass of some cloud of gas & dust, it must be both **dense** and **cold**.[+]
- Our galaxy's disc is filled with warm, low-density "neutral medium" ($H\text{ I}$, $T \sim 8000\text{ K}$, $n \sim 0.5\text{ cm}^{-3}$), but shocks (e.g. from SN) cause this to locally compress.[+]
- Cooling scales with $n^2$, so densified gas quickly cools, especially through emission from metal lines (e.g. $[\text{C II}] \,\, 158\,\mu\text{m}$ line).[+]
- Once a cloud is cold enough, atoms form molecules (e.g. H+H$\to$H$_2$, C+O$\to$CO$, helped by dust shielding UV), giving us their name: **molecular clouds**[+]

-v-
## Star formation in the Milky Way
- These dense ($n \sim 30\text{ cm}^{-3}$), cold ($T \sim 50\text{--}100\text{ K}$) environments have lower Jeans mass and can graviationally collapse.[+]
- Additional fragmentation through turbulence/etc typically split $\sim 1\text{--}2 \, M_\odot$ pre-stellar cores into the $\sim 0.1\text{--}0.5 \, M_\odot$ peak seen in the initial mass function (IMF).[+]

### First Star Formation
- **No metal cooling**: Without metal lines, which can cool gas to  $\sim 10\text{ K}$, zero-metal gas remains hot $T_{\text{min}} \sim 200\text{--}300\text{ K}$ with fast sound speed, which inhibits gas collapse at low mass.[+]
- **Population III IMF**: High thermal pressure raises the Jeans Mass ($M_J \propto T^{3/2} \rho^{-1/2}$), driving initial star formation dominated by extremely massive stars ($M_* \sim 10\text{--}100+ M_\odot$) - a "top-heavy" Initial Mass Function[+]
- **Nucleosynthetic Enrichment**: End states via Pair-Instability Supernovae (PISNe) and core-collapse events eject initial metals ($C, O, Mg, Fe$). [+]
- **Pop III $\to$ Pop II Transition**: Crossing critical metallicity ($Z_{\text{crit}} \approx 10^{-5.5}\text{--}10^{-4} \, Z_\odot$) enables dust/metal cooling, triggering low-mass fragmentation and normal IMF star formation. [+]

-v-

### A Gas-rich universe

- Dark matter filaments and halos (except those $M_{\text{halo}} \gt 10^{12} M_\odot$) accrete cold gas directingly into galaxy discs without shock heating.[+]
- This meant very high gas fractions, $f_{\text{gas}} \equiv M_{\text{gas}} / (M_* + M_{\text{gas}}) \sim 30\text{--}80\%$.[+]
- High surface gas density fragmented discs into into clumps (so-called **Toomre instability**), causing highly efficient star formation.[+]
- High galaxy densities also meant many "wet" mergers driving gas inflows. [+]

## Stellar Mass Density Accumulation

- Stellar Mass Growth ($\rho_*(z) = \int \psi(z) (1-R) \, dz$): [+]
  - Total stellar mass density $\rho_*(z)$ grows monotonically, reaching 50% of current local budget by $z \sim 1$. [+]
  - Mass return fraction ($R \approx 0.3\text{--}0.5$) accounts for mass returned to ISM via stellar winds and SNe. [+]
- Mass-Dependent Star Formation Histories ("Downsizing"): [+]
  - *Massive Galaxies ($M_ > 10^{11} M_\odot$):* Formed the bulk of their stars in early, intense starbursts at $z > 2$ and underwent rapid **quenching**. [+]
  - *Low-Mass Galaxies ($M_ < 10^9 M_\odot$):* Exhibit extended, gradual star-formation histories peaking late ($z < 0.5$). [+]

## Downsizing in Star Formation
- We observe that massive galaxies form the bulk of their stellar mass early and rapidly. [+]
- This star formation appears to shut-off rapidly, with no recent star formation in elliptical galaxies[+]
- Modern massive elliptical galaxies host extremely old, but also metal-rich stellar populations [+]
- In lower-mass galaxies star-formation is systematically shifted later.[+]

- One question is: why did the universe's most massive galaxies stop growing billions of years ago?[+]

Note:
- Specific SFR ($sSFR$): Ratio $sSFR = \text{SFR} / M_*$ declines universally, but remains elevated in low-mass systems at late times.
-v-

## Feedback Mechanisms

- Ongoing star formation impacts the availability of cold gas
- *Small galaxies* ($M_* \lesssim 10^{10.5} M_\odot$) - **Supernova-dominated**:[+]
  - Energy/momentum from core-collapse supernovae drives galactic winds ($v \sim 100\text{--}500\text{ km s}^{-1}$). [+]
  - Central Black Holes (AGNs) drives episodic outflows, especially during major accretion/merger events. [+]
- *Massive Ellipticals* ($M_* \gtrsim 10^{10.5} M_\odot)$) - **AGN-Dominated**:[+]
  - Radio-mode (kinetic / maintenance) and Quasar-mode (ejective) AGN feedback dominate feedback. [+]
  - High gravity means infalling cold gas is shock-heated to high temps ($T \sim 10^6\text{ K}$). [+]
  - Both mechanisms inject energy into hot halo gas, offsetting radiative cooling.[+]

## Quenching Paths: Blue Cloud to Red Sequence

[i=color_magnitude_plot.png]

- **Quenching** is the rapid or slow shutdown of star formation.[+]
- Observationally, this moves galaxies through the *Green Valley* to the passive *Red Sequence*. [+]


-v-

## 1) Quenching by Starvation: Cut off the Supply
- Feedback mechanisms mean a galaxy's outer reservoir of halo gas is stripped or prevented from cooling, cutting supply of cold gas. [+]
- Star formation continues uninterrupted, burning through the remaining internal cold molecular gas reservoir in the disk. [+]
- Star formation gradually dies over long timescales ($\sim 1\text{--}3\text{ Gyr}$). [+]
-v-

## 2) Morphological Quenching: Stabilizing the Gas Disk

- A large central bulge increases differential rotation and gravitational shear, quantified by the epicyclic frequency ($\kappa$). [+]
- **Toomre Criterion**: This high shear drives Toomre's stability parameter above the threshold for gravitational fragmentation ($Q = \frac{c_s \kappa}{\pi G \Sigma_{\text{gas}}} > 1$).[+]
- Cold gas remains present inside the disk, but strong gravitational shear prevents it from fragmenting into dense, starbursting clumps. [+]
 
-v-

## 3) Environmental Quenching: Satellite & Cluster Effects
- In dense cluster environments, interactions between galaxies results in quenching.
- As a satellite plunges through the dense hot Intra-Cluster Medium (ICM), wind drag overcomes galactic gravity and blows cold disk gas out through **Ram Pressure Stripping** [+]
- **Harassment & Tidal Stripping** is when high-speed flybys with neighbours & tidal forces within the cluster gravitationally tear gas out of the satellite. [+]
- This ejected cold gas on short timescales ($\lesssim 100\text{--}500\text{ Myr}$), leaving a passive satellite galaxy. [+]

Note:
- $$P_{\text{ram}} = \rho_{\text{ICM}} v^2 > F_{\text{grav}}$$[+]

### $[\alpha/\text{Fe}]$ Element Ratios as Cosmic Clocks
- Core-Collapse SNe (Type II) enrich ISM predominantly with $\alpha$-elements ($O, Mg, Si, Ca$) in $\lesssim 10\text{ Myr}$[+]
- Thermonuclear SNe (Type Ia) yield dominant iron-peak elements ($Fe, Ni$) but on longer timescales ($\sim 0.1\text{--}1\text{ Gyr}$). [+]
- Hence, **High $[\alpha/\text{Fe}]$** $=$ **Intense starburst** completed before Type Ia onset (e.g., massive ellipticals). [+]
- **Solar/Low $[\alpha/\text{Fe}]$** $=$ **Extended star formation** allowing Type Ia products to pollute gas (e.g., spiral disks). [+]
- $[\alpha/\text{Fe}]$ vs. $[\text{Fe}/\text{H}]$ therefore tracks star formation duration independent of direct lookback redshift. [+]

## Morphological & Structural Evolution
- Gas Kinematics: Structural shift from dispersion-dominated, turbulent, clumpy disks ($z \sim 2$) to settled, rotationally supported disks ($z \sim 0$). [+]
- Bulge Assembly: Driven by inward migration of giant disk clumps, major/minor mergers, and secular gas inflows. [+]
- Structural Transformation: Quenching strongly correlates with increased central stellar density ($\Sigma_1$) and high Sersic index ($n > 2.5$). [+]
- Bimodality Emergence: Clear structural, color ($U-V$), and star formation rate density bimodalities established across cosmic time. [+]

## Physical Origin & Redshift Invariance
- Self-Regulated Equilibrium: Driven by balance between pristine gas accretion, star formation, and metal-rich outflows. [+]
- Pristine Accretion Phase: Cold gas inflows dilute gas-phase metallicity while driving up $\text{SFR}$ (lowers $Z$, raises $\text{SFR}$). [+]

[p "The Cosmic Baryon Cycle & Gas Reservoirs"]
### Evolution of Cosmic Gas Reservoirs
- Atomic Gas ($\text{H I}$) vs. Molecular Gas ($\text{H}_2$): [+]
  - Atomic Gas ($\Omega_{\text{HI}}$): Probed by Damped Lyman-Alpha absorbers (DLAs); remains remarkably constant from $z \sim 4$ to $z = 0$. [+]
  - Molecular Gas ($\Omega_{\text{H2}}$): Probed by CO rotational transitions and dust continuum; strongly tracks $\psi(z)$, peaking at $z \sim 2$. [+]
- Gas Mass Fraction Evolution ($f_{\text{gas}} \equiv M_{\text{gas}} / (M_ + M_{\text{gas}})$):* [+]
  - High-$z$ star-forming disks are gas-rich ($f_{\text{gas}} \sim 50\text{--}80\%$ at $z \sim 2$). [+]
  - Local spiral disks are gas-poor ($f_{\text{gas}} \lesssim 10\text{--}20\%$). [+]
- Depletion Timescale ($\tau_{\text{dep}} \equiv M_{\text{gas}} / \text{SFR}$): [+]
  - Molecular gas depletion time stays relatively stable ($\tau_{\text{dep}} \sim 1\text{--}2\text{ Gyr}$) for main-sequence galaxies across redshift. [+]
  - High $\psi(z)$ at Cosmic Noon is driven by larger gas reservoirs ($f_{\text{gas}}$), not higher star formation efficiency per unit gas. [+]

## The Galactic Equilibrium Regulator Model

- Baryon Budget Flow Equations: [+]
  - $\frac{dM_g}{dt} = \dot{M}_{\text{in}} - \text{SFR} - \dot{M}_{\text{out}}$ [+]
- Mass Loading Factor ($\eta$): [+]
  - Quantifies outflow rates relative to star formation: $\dot{M}_{\text{out}} = \eta \cdot \text{SFR}$. [+]
Scales inversely with halo mass ($\eta \propto M_*^{-0.35}$); energy-driven winds eject gas efficiently from low-mass potential wells. [+]
Self-Regulation Regime: [+]
Galaxies act as open systems, rapidly adjusting their SFR to balance pristine gas inflows ($\dot{M}_{\text{in}}$) with gas consumption and outflows. [+]

[p "Chemical Enrichment & Metallicity Relations"]

Mass-Metallicity Relation (MZR) & EvolutionGas-Phase Mass-Metallicity Relation ($M_ - Z$):* [+]
Strong correlation between galaxy stellar mass $M_*$ and gas metallicity ($12 + \log(\text{O/H})$). [+]
Monotonic rise at low mass; flattens at $M_* \gtrsim 10^{10.5} M_\odot$ toward upper yield limits. [+]
Redshift Evolution of the MZR: [+]
At fixed $M_*$, gas metallicity drops systematically with increasing redshift ($\sim 0.3\text{--}0.5\text{ dex}$ drop from $z=0$ to $z \sim 2$). [+]
Driven by higher pristine gas inflow rates ($\dot{M}_{\text{in}}$) and stronger mass-loaded outflows at high $z$. [+]

-v-
The Fundamental Metallicity Relation (FMR)The FMR 3D Surface ($M_*, \text{SFR}, Z$): [+]
Metallicity correlates inversely with SFR at fixed stellar mass: $Z = f(M_* - \alpha \log \text{SFR})$ with $\alpha \approx 0.32$. [+]
Inflow Bursts: Fresh gas accretion drives up SFR while diluting ISM metallicity. [+]
Stagnant Phases: Low gas accretion leads to low SFR, allowing stellar nucleosynthesis to enrich the remaining gas. [+]
Redshift Invariance: [+]
Local and high-redshift galaxies ($z \lesssim 2.5$) lie on the exact same FMR surface, confirming metallicity is set by dynamic equilibrium between inflow, outflow, and SF. [+]

-v-

Radial Metallicity GradientsNegative Gradients in Disk Galaxies: [+]
Inner disk regions are systematically more metal-rich than outer disks ($d[\text{O/H}]/dR < 0$). [+]
Signature of inside-out disk assembly (higher historical star formation density in galaxy centers). [+]
Gradient Flattening Drivers: [+]
Bar-Driven Stirring: Central bars generate non-axisymmetric gravitational torques, forcing radial gas mixing. [+]
Galaxy Mergers: Tidal interactions drive massive radial gas inflows, flattening metallicity gradients prior to coalescence. [+]

$\alpha$-Elements (O, Mg, Si, S, Ca): Produced rapidly by Core-Collapse SNe (massive stars, timescales $\tau \lesssim 10^7\text{ yr}$). [+]

After $\sim 1\text{ Gyr}$, Type Ia SNe turn on, injecting Fe and driving $[\alpha/\text{Fe}]$ down to solar values ($0.0$). [+]

# Spheroid vs. Disk Star Formation Timelines

Alpha-Enhancement in Giant Ellipticals: [+]
Massive spheroids exhibit enhanced abundances ($[\alpha/\text{Fe}] \sim +0.2\text{--}+0.3$). [+]
Proves star formation was truncated rapidly ($\Delta t_{\text{SF}} < 1\text{ Gyr}$) before Type Ia SNe could enrich the ISM with iron. [+]
Solar $[\alpha/\text{Fe}]$ in Disk Galaxies: [+]
Spiral disks exhibit near-solar ratios ($[\alpha/\text{Fe}] \approx 0.0$). [+]
Reflects extended, continuous star formation histories ($\Delta t_{\text{SF}} \gg 1\text{ Gyr}$) with fully integrated Type Ia feedback. [+]

Classical Chemical Evolution ModelsClosed-Box Model: [+]
Assumes no gas inflow or outflow; yields $Z(t) = -p \ln f_{\text{gas}}$ (where $p$ is stellar yield). [+]
Failure Mode: Overpredicts low-metallicity stars in local disks ("G-Dwarf Problem"). [+]
Leaky-Box Model (Outflows): [+]
Incorporates wind ejection ($\dot{M}_{\text{out}} = \eta \cdot \text{SFR}$); reduces effective yield to $p_{\text{eff}} = \frac{p}{1+\eta}$. [+]
Successfully reproduces low metallicities in dwarf galaxies. [+]
Accreting-Box / Inflow Model: [+]
Continuous accretion of pristine gas buffers ISM metallicity at a steady state ($Z \to \frac{p}{1+\eta}$); naturally solves the G-Dwarf Problem in disks. [+]-->
