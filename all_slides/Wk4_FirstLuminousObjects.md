### Star Formation Rates

- Typically traced by massive short-lived stars:[+]

[P "Recombination"]

## Ionization basics
- Neutral Atom: Protons in the nucleus hold negatively charged electrons in bound orbits. [+]
- Ionization Energy: Stripping an electron from Hydrogen requires a minimum energy of $13.6\text{ eV}$ (from $E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$; $\lambda<911{\text \AA}$)[+]
- Ionization Process: High-energy Ultraviolet (UV) photons strike neutral Hydrogen ($H\ I$), ejecting the electron. [+]
- Ionized State: Gas converts into a plasma of free protons and free electrons ($H\ II$). [+]

-v-

## The CMB
- The early universe was **hot, dense, & expanding**[+]
- High temperatures meant electrons were initially unable to combine with nuclei/protons (**ionized plasma**)[+]
- **Thomson scattering** with the sea of electrons meant photons had very short *mean free paths*[+]
- Any local fluctuations in density are dispersed by radiation pressure setting up sound waves (**"Baryon Acoustic Oscillations"**)[+]
- The expanding universe cooled such that fewer than 1 photon per baryon had $E>13.6\text{ eV}$ (or $\lambda<911{\text \AA}$)[+]
- Free electons could bind with protons to form neutral Hydrogen ($H\ I$) without being reionized[+]
- The disappearance of free electrons meant photon *mean free paths* grew rapidly to $\ell \gg H^{-1}$, releasing photons we can still observe as the **cosmic microwave background (CMB)**[+]

## Neutral era "Cosmic Dawn"
- Neutral $H\ I$ is transparent to photons...[+]
  - Except photons with $\lambda<911{\text \AA}$ which are absorbed through ionization ($H\ I \to H^{+} + e^{-}$).[+]
  - Accounting for cosmic red-shift, this means we cannot expect *any* light $<1\mu{\rm m}$ from the universe beyond $z \gtrsim 10$[+]
- However, in this initial cooling universe, there are almost no sources of photons.[+]
  - These are the **Cosmic Dark Ages**[+]
  - Only the 21cm line shines (e$^{-}$ & H$^{+}$ spin-flip transition)[+]

### Gravitational Collapse
- Erly universe was remarkable smooth (see inflation)[+]
- But small-scale fluctuations in dark matter gradually clump into structures (even before recombination).[+]
- Densest regions form at the intersection of filaments - so-called **mini-halos** with masses of $\sim 10^6\text{ M}_\odot$. [+]
  - Predicted by simulations; see part 5. [+]
- By $z\sim20$ neutral gas falls into these gravitation wells at speeds of $v_{\text{vir}} \sim 2\text{--}5\text{ km/s}$, heating the gas via shocks to $T_{\text{vir}} \sim 1000\text{ K}$.[+]

[P "The first stars & galaxies"]

### Forming population III Stars - Cooling
- Low $T_{\text{vir}}$ blocks efficient Ly$\alpha$ cooling ($10.2\text{ eV}$ requires $T_{\text{vir}} \gtrsim 10^4\text{ K}$)[+]
- Lack of heavy elements means neutral H cannot cool below 1000K[+]
- Residual free electrons helps form H$_2$ molecules (small fraction), allowing rotational transitions to radiate heat ($1000\text{ K} \to 200\text{ K}$; $n_H \to \sim 10^4\text{ cm}^{-3}$).[+]
- H$\_{2}$ cooling means the local Jeans mass ($M_J \propto T^{3/2} \rho^{-1/2}$) drops to $M_J \sim 10^2\text{--}10^3 \, M_\odot$ - self-gravitational collapse can begin.[+]
- This may form multiple smaller dense regions via turbulence.[+]
- At $n_H \sim 10^8\text{ cm}^{-3}$ convert all H$\to$H$\_2$ (via $3H \to H_2 + H$ pathway).[+]

-v-
### Forming population III Stars - Accretion
- At $n_H \sim 10^{14}\text{ cm}^{-3}$, gas is optically thick to $H_2$ rovibrational lines and the core temperature rises adiabatically, forming a protostellar core ($M_{\text{proto}} \sim 10^{-2} \, M_\odot$).[+]
- Pristine gas at 200K has a very high sound speed ($c_s \approx 1.5\text{--}2.0\text{ km/s}$) enabling rapid accretion (1-10M_sun in 1000yrs!)[+]
- Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction during the accretion phase means the core gets hot & dense enough to begin Hydrogen fusion[+]
- The emitted UV radiation (Ly$\alpha$ continuum) photoevaporates the surrounding accreting material, switching off accretion.[+]

-v-

### Forming population III Stars - How many?

- A $10^6\text{ M}_\odot$ halo:
  - Contains $M_{\rm gas} \sim 1.6\times10^5\text{ M}_\odot$ neutral hydrogen[+]
  - Accretion efficiency of $$\epsilon_{\text{SF}} \approx 10^{-4} \text{ to } 10^{-3}$$(0.01 to 0.1%) due to photo-evaporative feedback.[+]
  - $$N_{\text{stars}} = \frac{\epsilon_{\text{SF}} . M_{\rm gas}}{M_{\text{Pop III}}} \approx \frac{1.6 \times 10^5 .(10^{-4} \text{ -- } 10^{-3})}{20 \text{--}100 \, M_\odot} \approx 1 \text{ to } 8 \text{ stars}$$ [+]
  - Each halo produces only one or a handful of Pop III stars![+]

-v-

### Evolutionary Paths & Explosive Ends - low-mass

- Pop III star fates depend strictly on initial mass ($M_{\text{ZAMS}}$):
- $10\ M_{\text{ZAMS}} \lesssim M_{\text{ZAMS}} \lesssim 140\ M_{\odot}$ — **Core-Collapse Supernovae (CCSN)**:[+]
  - 3-10Myr main sequence lifetime[+]
  - Standard iron-core collapse leaving a neutron star or black hole via fallback.[+]
  - Smallest stars ($<30\ M_{\odot}$) enriches the environment with $\sim80$% up to Fe[+]
  - Larger stars only minimally enriches the environment ($\sim20$%) with alpha elements (C, N, O).[+]

-v-

### Evolutionary Paths & Explosive Ends - intermediate-mass

- $140\ M_{\odot} \le M_{\text{ZAMS}} \le 260\ M_{\odot}$ — **Pair-Instability Supernovae (PISNe)**:[+]
  - 2-3Myr main sequence lifetime[+]
  - Photon energies in the oxygen core become so high that they create electron-positron ($e^-e^+$) pairs, dropping thermal pressure and causing catastrophic collapse.[+]
  - Triggers runaway explosive oxygen and silicon burning that completely disrupts the star, leaving no central compact remnant.[+]
  - Massively enriches the surrounding gas, including in $^{56}$Ni which decays to Fe ($1\text{--}10$% by mass)[+]

-v-

### Evolutionary Paths & Explosive Ends - high-mass

- $M_{\text{ZAMS}} \gtrsim 260\ M_{\odot}$ — **Direct Collapse to Black Hole**:[+]
  - <2Myr main sequence lifetime[+]
  - Photodisintegration steals thermal energy, leading to collapse that runaway fusion cannot halt. The star collapses directly into an intermediate-mass black hole (IMBH). [+]
  - Little to no mass is ejected (minimal enrichment)[+]

-v-

### The resulting Dark Matter Halo

- Supernova, with winds of $1,000+\text{ km/s}$ blows all gas out of mini-halo into IGM[+]
- Dark Matter (and black hole) remain trapped within gravitational well[+]
- The halo enters a **sterile phase** with no gas or star formation for $>100$ Myr.[+]

-v-

### Hierarchical Merging
- The Dark Matter mass continues to grow through interactions with both **filaments**, and other halos. [+]
- Assembles via **Hierarchical Merging** to masses of $M_{\text{halo}} \gtrsim 10^8\text{--}10^9 \, M_\odot$.[+]
- Once the potential well grows deep enough ($v_{\text{esc}} > 10\text{--}15\text{ km/s}$), its gravity re-accretes gas, albeit heated by e.g. shocks to $T_{\text{vir}} \ge 10^4\text{ K}$[+]
- Unlike for Pop III formation, this gas is dominated by molecular hydrogen, enriched with metals from supernovae, and has $T_{\text{vir}}$ hot enough to cool via Ly$\alpha$ emission, enabling efficient cooling. [+]
- This unlocks rapid gas accretion from the IGM, and from narrow streams of gas which channel directly into central protogalactic disc.[+]
- This forms the first true galaxies, with gas-rich compositions, and rapid starbursts forming Population II stars.[+]

[P "Simulating the Cosmic Dark Ages"]

- Without direct observations, theory & simulations are an important tool to understand how star & galaxy formation works.[+]
- This is especially true for understanding how matter & dark matter produce structures.[+]
- By simulating the cosmic dark ages, and comparing the aftermath with what we can observe in the distant universe, we can constrain underlying physics.[+]

-v-

## Tunable Physical Parameters
- **Cosmological Parameters** Density parameters ($\Omega_m, \Omega_b, \Omega_\Lambda$), Hubble constant ($h$), power spectrum amplitude ($\sigma_8$), and scalar spectral index ($n_s$).[+]
- **Dark Matter Microphysics**: Particle mass ($m_{\text{WDM}}$ in Warm Dark Matter models to set the small-scale power suppression scale), self-interaction cross-sections ($\sigma_{\text{SIDM}}$).[+]
- **Gas Microphysics & Primordial Chemistry**: Non-equilibrium network reaction rates for species ($H, H^+, H^-, He, He^+, He^{++}, e^-, H_2, H_2^+$), molecular hydrogen ($H_2$) and HD cooling efficiencies.[+]
- **Stellar Formation Thresholds**: Critical $H_2$ fraction, density threshold for collapse ($\rho_{\text{crit}} \sim 10^4\, \text{cm}^{-3}$), and the initial mass function (IMF) range of Population III stars ($10–1000 \, M_\odot$)[+]

-v-

## Benchmark Projects

- The **First Stars** Simulations (Abel, Bryan, Norman / Bromm et al. late 90s): Pioneer high-resolution collapse simulations establishing Pop III star formation in DM mini-haloes.[+]
- The **Millenium Simulation** (Springel 2005): $10^{10}$ purely dark matter particles. Updated in 2009 at higher resolution.[+]
[i=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yyfpFfWq7Bc]

## Benchmark Projects
- The **Renaissance Simulations** (Enzo): Ultra-high-resolution Adaptive Mesh Refinement (AMR) simulations exploring Population III/II star formation & early galaxy assembly. Suggested that low-mass, ultra-faint dwarf galaxies supplied most ionizing photons.[+]
- **CoDa** (Cosmic Dawn) Series (RAMSES-CORTEX): Fully coupled GPU-accelerated radiation-hydrodynamics simulations tracking the joint evolution of gas, stars, and ionizing radiation field.[+]
- **Flamingo** (Schaye et al. 2023): Largest-scale simulations (2.8Gpc) directly constraining upcoming survey observations (Euclid, Rubin, DESI, Roman)[+]

[P "The Epoch of Reionization (EoR)"]

## The Three Phases of Reionization

- Phase 1: **Bubble Formation**: [+]
  - The first Pop III stars are embedded in neutral $H\ I$ gas.[+]
  - Their extremely high temperatures produce UV photons ionize immediate surroundings. [+]
  - The first generations of Pop II stars in proto-galaxies also contribute to these UV photons.[+]
  - Creates isolated spheres of $H\ II$ gas inside a vast ocean of neutral $H\ I$. [+]
- Phase 2: **Overlap (Percolation)**: [+]
  - Galaxies grow denser; ionized bubbles expand rapidly and merge together. [+]
  - Ionizing photon supply exceeds the recombination rate of the Intergalactic Medium (IGM). [+]

-v-

## The Three Phases of Reionization

- Phase 3: **Post-Overlap** ($z \sim 5.5\text{--}6$): [+]
  - Reionization finishes approximately 1 billion years after the Big Bang. [+]
  - The Intergalactic Medium becomes fully ionized ($>99.9\%$). [+]
- UV radiation can now travel freely across cosmic distances without being absorbed by neutral hydrogen. [+]

### 21cm line observations

- The spin-flip transition in neutral hydrogen ($\text{H I}$) emits or absorbs photons at $21.1\text{ cm}$ ($\nu_0 = 1420\text{ MHz}$)[+]
- Broad emission of radio photons, we can measure the differential brightness temperature ($\delta T_b$) of the $21\text{ cm}$ relative to the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB):[+]
  - **Absorption** ($\delta T_b < 0$) when neutral gas absorbs CMB photons[+]
  - **Emission** ($\delta T_b > 0$): when heated gas glows brighter than the CMB background.[+]
- $21\text{ cm}$ transition is optically thin ($\tau \ll 1$; unlike Lyman-alpha forest)[+]
- Reionization of $H\ I$ by UV (e.g. produced by the first stars) is predicted to cause cooling of its spin temperature, producing an absorption signature.[+]

## The Photon Budget Problem

- To reionize the universe, ionizing photon production rate ($\dot{n}_{\text{ion}}$) must overcome both the neutral hydrogen density and the rate at which ionized gas recombines.[+]
- For reionization to complete by $z \sim 6$, the universe requires 1 -- 3 ionizing photons per hydrogen atom.[+]
- Initially, observations could not see enough ionizing photons from the identified galaxies/quasars to ionize the univere by $z\sim5$[+]
- Suggested we were missing an ultra-faint population of dwarf galaxies...[+]
- JWST detected this population, flipping the photon budget problem[+]
  - Now have *too many* UV photons to reionize after $z\sim8$[+]
