<!--# What do we see in Spiral Galaxies?-->
[P "What do we see in Spiral Galaxies?"]

### Inclination
[i=spiral_pair.jpg]

- Spiral galaxies show face-on or edge-on geometries - they are **flat discs** - i.e. like the Milky Way [+]

-v-

### Milky Way Structure
- **Thin Disk**: Scale height $h_z \approx 300\text{ pc}$, young Population I stars, high gas fraction, active star formation.[+]
- **Thick Disk**: Scale height $h_z \approx 1\text{ kpc}$, older metal-poor stars, higher velocity dispersion.[+]
- **Central Bulge**: Spheroidal concentration of stars in the core ($R \lesssim 1\text{--}2\text{ kpc}$).[+] 
- **Stellar (& Dark Matter) Halo**: Metal-poor stars, globular clusters, extended dark matter mass distribution.[+] 

-v-

### Arms
[i="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c5/M101_hires_STScI-PRC2006-10a.jpg"]

- Spiral number:
  - Regular spirals typically have 2 distinct arms - **grand design spirals**[+]
  - Sometimes spiral patterns do not cover the full disc or are incoherent ("intermediate" spirals)[+]
- We can measure the average angle of the arms with respect to perpendicular from the centre[+]
- Very low pitch angles are called **wound-up spirals**[+]

-v-
### Arms
[i=spiral-galaxy-ngc-2841.jpg]

- Very occasionally, there are a "sea" of spiral-like structures without global symmetry - **flocculant spirals**[+]
- Driven by stochastic self-propagating star formation[+]
-v-
### Arms
- We see that stars do not rotate with constant angular velocity - more distant stars have slower angular velocities:[+]
  - $$\Omega(r) = \frac{v_c(r)}{r}; \Omega(r) \propto 1/r$$[+]
- What does this imply for spiral arms?[+]
  - Spirals moving with the stars would quickly become more and more wound (the so-called **winding problem**)[+]
  - So spiral arms are not comoving with the stars.[+]

-v-

###  Bulges
[i="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/98/M31_center_HST.jpg"]

- Almost all spiral galaxies show a bulge
-v-
### Classical Bulges
- Spherical, pressure-supported (ratio of rotational velocity $V$ and velocity dispersion $\sigma_V$, $V/\sigma_V < 1$).[+]
- Usually show modest or absent (i.e. random) rotation[+]
- Formed rapidly via early major mergers or monolithic collapse.[+]
- Steep surface brightness profiles ($n \approx 4$).[+]
- Typically yellow/white in colour[+]
-v-
### Pseudobulges (Disk-like):
- Flattened, rotationally supported ($V/\sigma > 1$)[+]
- Formed slowly via secular internal disk instabilities and bar driving.[+]
- Exponential-like surface brightness profiles ($n \approx 1\text{--}2$).[+]
- Some bulges show strong rotation are called "pseudobulges"[+]
- Appear like small elliptical galaxies (see later)[+]

-v-

### Bars

[i=ngc1300_hst_big.jpg]

- Linear stellar structures spanning galaxy centers, found in $\sim 65\%$ of disk galaxies
- Bar axis is aligned along the plane of the galactic disc
- Most obvious in NIR (than e.g. UV)

-v-

### Rings
[i=ring_galaxies.png]

-v-
### Rings
  - Nuclear Rings ($nr$): Located near the Inner Lindblad Resonance (ILR, $r \lesssim 1\text{ kpc}$), hosting intense circumnuclear starbursts. [+]
  - Inner Rings ($r$): Encircle the bar ends at the Corotation Radius (CR), defining the edge of the inner disk. [+]
  - Outer Rings ($R$ / $R_1 R_2'$): Faint, large-scale structures situated near the Outer Lindblad Resonance (OLR, $r \sim 2.5 R_{\text{bar}}$[+]
  - Collisional Ring Galaxies: Formed via collisions (e.g., Cartwheel Galaxy)[+]

# Galaxies are complex 3D objects - how do we classify them?

# Qualitative vs quantitative?
[P "Galaxy classification - qualitative"]
-v-
# Galaxy classification - qualitative

- First introduced in 1926 (with many attempts since to update it)
- Four classes:
- - Ellipticals (E0 -> E7)
- - Lenticulars (S0 & SB0)
- - Spirals (Sa/b/c or SBa/b/c)
- - Irregulars (Irr)

-v-

- These categories are determined by:
- **Symmetricality**:[+]
  - Assymetric = irregular[+]
- **Concentration of light** as a function of distance:[+]
  - Sharp decay (& featureless) = Elliptical[+]
  - Extended "flat" disc = Lenticular or Spiral[+]
- **Observed shape** (for ellipticals only):[+]
  - Oblateness from circular ($a=b$; E0) to ($a=0.3*b$; E7)[+]
- **Presence of spiral arms** (S) and/or bar (SB)[+]
  - Absence of spirals (Lenticular, 0)[+]
  - Tight spirals & large central core (a) -> open spirals & small central core (c)[+]

-v-

## The "Tuning fork"
[i=tuning-fork-diagram-of-hubble.jpg]

- Also called "Early" (ETGs) to "Late" galaxies (LTGs) (mirroring stars)[+]
- This does not really imply evolution _along_ the diagram![+]

-v-
## de Vacouleurs revision (1959)
[i=hubble-de-Vaucouleurs-Galaxy-Morphology-Diagram.jpg]

-v-
## de Vacouleurs revision (1959)
- Addition of "A" to "normal" spirals (SA), as well as "intermediate" (SAB) between normal & barred.[+]
- Subscript letters "r" and "s" after class denotes "ring" or "spiral"[+]
- Superscript "+" or "-" denotes early-ness or late-ness for E (elliptical) and S0 (lenticular) types.[+]
- Addition of "m" sub-class to denote magellanic-like small/irrelegular (e.g. SAm or Im)[+]
- e.g. SB(r)ab = barred spiral with inner ring and tightly wound arms[+]
-v-

## T-types
- Numerical galaxy classes from early to late:[+]
- Early types *from -5 (E0) to 0 (S0a)* (via E$^{+}$, S0$^{-}$, S0, S0$^{+}$)[+]
  - Ellipticals -6 to -4, Lenticulars -3 to -1.[+]
- Late type *from 0 (S0a) to +10 (Im)* (via Sa, Sab, Sb, Sbc, Sc, Scd, Sd, Sdm, Sm)[+]
- We will see more parametric ways of classifying galaxies later...[+]

<!--# Galaxy classification - quantitative-->

[P "Galaxy classification - quantitative"]

### Surface Brightness Profiles

[ism=isophate.jpg]

- How does the brightness (typically in ${\rm mag}.{\rm arcsec}^{-2}$) vary with distance from the center, $\mu_\lambda(R)$?[+]
- Ellipses connecting points of equal surface brightness - **Isophate**[+]
-v-
### Surface Brightness Profiles
- Isophotes are fit with concentric ellipses defined by center $(x_c, y_c)$, position angle ($\text{PA}$), and ellipticity ($\epsilon = 1 - b/a$). [+]
- Isophotal Deviations (e.g. using 4th fourier coefficient $a_4$): [+]
  - Boxy Isophotes: $a_4/a < 0$ (common in radio-loud, merger-remnant ellipticals). [+]
  - Disky Isophotes: $a_4/a > 0$ (uncovered embedded stellar disks). [+]

-v-
## Scale Radii & Half-Light Radius ($R_e$)

[ism=500px-Half_light_radius_simple.png]

- Surface Brightness Conversion: [+]
  - $\mu(R) = m_{\text{zero}} + 2.5 \log_{10} \left(\frac{\text{Area}}{\text{Flux}(R)} \right)$[+]
- $m_{\text{zero}}$: Photometric Zero-Point (calibration constant representing magnitude of an object producing $1\text{ count/second}$)[+]
- The **effective**, or **half-light radius** ($R_e$ or $R_{50}$): Radius enclosing $50\%$ of the galaxy's total integrated light. [+]
- Disk Scale Length ($h_R$): The exponential e-folding radius of a galaxy disk profile. [+]
-  ($R_e$) is defined by the isophate containing 50% of the total flux[+]

-v-

### Surface Brightness Profiles - Sérsic Profile

- We want a single value to compare two galaxies $\mu_\lambda(R)$ curves.
- $\mu_\lambda(R) = \mu_\lambda(Re) \exp{(-b(n)[\left(\frac{R}{R_e}\right)^{1/n}-1])}$.[+]
- Here, -b(n) is simply a normalisation term such that the sum of the flux inside and outside $R_e$ is equal ($b(n) = 2n - 1/3 + 4/(405n)$).[+]
- The **Sérsic Index ($n$)**: Quantifies central concentration. [+]
- $n = 4$: de Vaucouleurs profile (classical ellipticals/bulges). [+]
- $n = 1$: Exponential profile (pure disk systems). [+]
- $n=0.5$ is Gaussian $\exp{-R^2}$[+]
- The Sérsic profile of a galaxy may change with wavelength[+]
-v-
### Surface Brightness Profiles - Sérsic Fitting

[i=sersic_fitting.jpg]

-v-
### Surface Brightness Profiles - Sérsic Fitting

- Ellipticals show steep Sersic profiles ($2.5<n<10$)[+]
- For spirals, generally $n\sim1$ (i.e. "shallow" Sérsic profiles)[+]
- An n=1 Sersic profile = linear decrease in surface brightness (in magnitudes) as a function of radius (normalised to the disc scalelength, $R_d$): $\mu(R) = \mu_0 + 1.0857\frac{R}{R_d}[+]
- Central surface brightness then probes total galactic brightness[+]
- Galactic inclination, and any galactic bulge (Sersic profile n>1) must be corrected/removed.[+]

-v-

### Bulge-to-Disc ratio
- Spirals may have two components - a steep central bulge and a flatter disc - which compete in a single Sérsic profile.
- Typically classical bulges may have $n\sim4$ while pseudobulges have $n~1-2$
- **Bulge-to-disc** (B/D) or **bulge-to-total** (B/T) measurements can also be derived from a galaxy's surface brightness profile
- Quantitatively, B/T vaires with spiral class:

|  | S0/a | Sb | Scd |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | ---: |
| B/T | 0.2-0.3 | 0.1-0.2 | <0.1 |

-v-

### Color-Magnitude
- Galaxy colours and intrinsic luminosity fundamentally divide spirals and ellipticals
- Ellipticals and lenticulars: redder, and typically larger/brighter
- Spirals: Bluer (as active.star forming; $u-r < 1.8$), lower mass/luminosity.
- Intermediate transitional population undergoing star formation "quenching" (Green Valley). [+]

-v-

### "CAS" parameters / structures
- **Concentration ($C$)**: Ratio of flux within $80\%$ radius to $20\%$ radius: [+]
  - $C = 5 \log_{10} \left( \frac{r_{80}}{r_{20}} \right)$[+]
- **Asymmetry ($A$)**: Normalized residual flux after rotating galaxy image by $180^\circ$: [+]
  - $A = \frac{\sum \vert{}I_0 - I_{180}\vert{}}{2 \sum \vert{}I_0\vert{}}$[+]
- **Smoothness ($S$)**: Measure of high-spatial-frequency clumpiness (star-forming knots): [+]
  - $S = \frac{\sum \vert{}I_0 - I_s\vert{}}{\sum \vert{}I_0\vert{}}$[+]
-v-
### "CAS" parameters / structures

[i=CAS_metrics.png]

-v-

### Non-parametric metrics
- Directly using **pixel values** (rather than e.g. fitting ellipses)[+]
  - Traces spatial off-center brightness concentrations (multiple nuclei, bar endpoints, tidal knots).[+]
- $G$, **Gini coefficient** of pixels (bulge/core distribution):[+]
  - $G \approx 1$: Flux is concentrated in very few bright pixels (nucleated/AGN/steep ellipticals). [+]
  - $G \approx 0$: Flux is evenly distributed across all pixels (low surface brightness disk). [+]
- $M_{20}$ - second order of "moment of light" calculated from brightest 20% of flux (probes irregularity of core)[+]
([Lotz et al](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1086/421849/pdf))[+]
-v-
### The $G - M_{20}$ Morphological Plane
[i=compare_morph_gini_vs_morph_m20.png]

- Separates normal hubble types from dynamically disrupted systems without assuming structural symmetry. [+]
- Classification Domains: [+]
  - Ellipticals: High $G$, Low $M_{20}$. [+]
  - Spirals / Disks: Intermediate $G$, Moderate $M_{20}$. [+]
  - Mergers / Interactions: High $G$, High $M_{20}$ (distinct line separator: $G > -0.14 M_{20} + 0.33$). [+]

### Spiral galaxy rotation
- $$V_c(R) = \sqrt{\frac{G M(<R)}{R}}$$ - velocity set by enclose mass[+]
- Expectation from star/gas mass: $$V_c(R) \propto R^{-1/2}$$, i.e. velocities drop with distance.[+]
- Reality: rotation curves are **flat** throughout the visible disc[+]

<!--# Stars, stellar populations & star formation-->
[P "Stars, stellar populations & star formation"]

- The mass of stars determines both their **luminosity**, which also directly determine's their lifetime:[+]
  - $\frac{L_s}{L_\odot} \approx \frac{M_s}{M_\odot}^\alpha$.[+]
  - $\alpha=4$ for GK stars \& $\alpha=3.5$ for large main sequence stars (OBAF stars).[+]
  - As luminosity probes the rate of nuclear fusion, it is inversely proportional to stellar main sequence lifetime, $\tau_{MS} = 10^{10}{\rm yrs}.\frac{M_s}{M_\odot}\frac{L_\odot}{L_s}$ [+]
  - Stars also get hotter with mass, increase by around $\frac{L_s}{L_\odot} \approx \frac{M_s}{M_\odot}^0.525$.[+]
-v-
## Stellar Populations in Spiral Galaxies

[i="disc_halo_stars.jpg"]

- **Walter Baade’s Dichotomy (1944):** Stars in spiral galaxies divide into chemical, kinematic, & spatial populations: [+]
-v-
## Stellar Populations in Spiral Galaxies

#### Population I (Young & Metal-Rich):

* Solar to super-solar metallicity($[\text{Fe/H}] \sim -0.5 \text{ to } +0.3$)[+]
* Young: $< 8\text{ Gyr}$. [+]
* Concentrated in the **thin disk** ($\text{scale height } z_d \sim 300\text{ pc}$) & spiral arms. [+]
* Planar rotation & low velocity dispersion ($V_\phi \gg \sigma_z$). [+]
-v-
## Stellar Populations in Spiral Galaxies

#### Population II (Old & Metal-Poor):

* Low metallicity ($[\text{Fe/H}] \lesssim -1.0$)[+]
* Old $> 10\text{--}12\text{ Gyr}$. [+]
* Found in the **stellar halo**, globular clusters, and classical bulge. [+]
* Pressure-supported, highly eccentric orbits & large vertical dispersion ($\sigma_z \approx \sigma_R$). [+]
-v-
## Stellar Populations in Spiral Galaxies

#### Population III (Primordial):

* Pure primordial gas ($Z = 0$, $H$ and $He$ only) formed very early $z \gtrsim 10\text{--}20$. [+]
* Required massive stars ($10\text{--}100+ M_\odot$) that died rapidly via Pair-Instability or Core-Collapse SNe. [+]
* Extinct today - seeded Pop II stars with initial metals ($[\text{Fe/H}] \sim -4\text{ to } -3$). [+]
-v-
## Stellar Populations in Spiral Galaxies
- The Milky Way bar & bulge are yellow-white due to Pop II stars
- Color gradients in nearby spirals (e.g., M31, M101) also trace this population structure: blue star-forming Pop I arms and a red Pop II spheroid halo. [+]

-v-

### Star formation impact on galaxies

- Galaxies with active star formation must be **bluer** than galaxies which only have old stars.
- Spirals also have **spectroscopic** signatures of young stellar populations:
  - Young, hot stars show emission lines
  - GKM stars show a strong "break" at 4000\o{A} (**D4000 break**)
-v-
### Star formation impact on galaxies

[i=spiral_density_waves.gif]

- Blue star-forming regions of spiral galaxies are in the **spiral arms**
- This is because spiral arms are stable regions of **high density**, concentrating stars but also gas & dust

-v-
### Starburst galaxies
- Defined as galaxies undergoing **extreme star formation**
- Identified via colours, as well as strong emission lines in spectra[+]
- These are from 25000-50000K "Wolf-Rayet" stars larger than $20M_{\odot}$ which live only a few Myr[+]
- The *Gas Depletion Timescale* of such galaxies is also short $$\tau_{\text{dep}} = \frac{M_{\text{gas}}}{\text{SFR}} < 10^8 \text{ yr}$$[+]
- Drivers: Major/minor mergers, strong bar shocks, or extreme gas accretion events[+]
- Feedback mechanisms (e.g. supernovae & gas outflows during starbursts) supress star formation[+]

[P "Rare & Diffuse galaxies"]

### Low surface-brightness galaxies

- Some galaxies show very low surface brightness ($\mu_0(B)>23$ mag arcsec$^-2$)
- Velocities show they are unusually dark matter-dominated at all radii.
- Low star formation efficiency & low surface density of gas.
- Examples: Malin 1 (giant LSB galaxy with a giant low-density disk extending to $R > 100\text{ kpc}$).

-v-
### Ultra-Diffuse Galaxies (UDGs)

- Extreme Structural Parameters: 
  - Milky Way-sized effective radii ($R_e > 1.5\text{ kpc}$) but dwarf-like stellar masses ($M_* \sim 10^7\text{--}10^8 M_\odot$). [+]
  - Central Surface Brightness: $\mu_0(g) > 24\text{ mag/arcsec}^2$ (lower than LSBs). [+]
- Formation Scenarios: [+]
  - High-spin dwarf halos that expanded during formation. [+]
  - Failed $L^*$ galaxies stripped of their gas early in cluster environments. [+]

<!--# Galaxies in surveys-->

[P "Galaxies in surveys"]

#### Imaging surveys

- **Hubble Space Telescope (HST)**:[+]
  - High-resolution deep fields (HDF, HUDF, CANDELS) established high-redshift galaxy morphology out to $z > 3$. [+]
- **Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS)**:[+]
  - Wavelength coverage across $u,g,r,i,z$ over $35\%$ of the sky. Mapped local universe galaxy demographics ($N > 10^6$ spectra). [+]
  - First mapped the "blue cloud" and "red sequence"[+]
- **Dark Energy Camera (DECam / DES)**:[+]
  - Wide-field precursor imaging mapping low surface brightness tidal features and stellar halos. [+]
-v-

#### Spectroscopic surveys

- **Single-Fiber Spectroscopy** (SDSS-I/II, 2dFGRS):
  - Single fiber targeted at galaxy core[+]
  - Global redshift $z$, central stellar velocity dispersion $\sigma$, & nuclear line ratios... but misses outer disk properties.[+]
- **Multi-Object Spectroscopy** (MOS: DEEP2, VVDS, DESI, PFS):[+]
  - Robots align thousands of fibers simultaneously across a wide FoV.[+]
  - Maps 3D cosmic web structure, and galaxy clustering out to $z > 3$.[+] 
- **Integral Field Unit (IFU)** Spectroscopy (MaNGA, CALIFA, SAMI):[+]
  - Fibre bundles or pixel prisms (Integral Field Units) - spatially resolved 2D maps (spaxels) of velocity, age, & metallicity.[+]

-v-

### Next-Generation Astronomical Surveys

[i="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e8/Euclid_Spacecraft_illustration.png"]

- **Euclid Space Mission (ESA)**: [+]
  - Wide-field diffraction-limited optical + NIR space imaging ($15,000\text{ deg}^2$). [+]
  - Tracing morphological transformation and weak lensing shear for billions of distant galaxies. [+]
- **Vera C. Rubin Observatory (LSST)**: [+]
  - $8.4\text{m}$ ground-based wide-field synoptic survey ($18,000\text{ deg}^2$). [+]
  - Reaches surface brightness limits $\mu_r \sim 32\text{ mag/arcsec}^2$, revealing ultra-faint dwarfs and streams. [+]
- **Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope (NASA)**: Infrared survey space telescope with $100\times$ HST's field of view. [+]

## Deep Learning & Convolutional Neural Networks

[i="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/63/Typical_cnn.png"]

- The Machine Learning Shift: Scaling profile fitting to $> 10^9$ galaxies from Rubin/Euclid requires neural networks. [+]
- **Convolutional Neural Networks**: [+]
  - Input: Raw multi-band pixel cutouts ($u,g,r,i,z$). [+]
  - Output: Probabilities for morphological features (bars, arm spiral tightness, mergers). [+]
- **Unsupervised Machine Learning**:[+]
  - Autoencoders & self-supervised algorithms 
  - Group galaxy images without pre-existing human taxonomies. [+]

<!--# Key Scientific Discoveries from Modern Surveys-->
[P "Key Scientific Discoveries from Modern Surveys"]

[i="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b5/Morphology_density_relation.png"]

- **Morphology-Density Relation**: Disk galaxies dominate low-density field environments, whereas dense cluster cores are dominated by S0 and Elliptical galaxies (Dressler 1980). [+]
- **Secular Structural Growth**: Bars and spiral density waves account for up to 30% of central bulge mass assembly since $z \sim 1$. [+]
- JWST has revealed well-formed, rotationally supported spirals occurring much earlier ($z > 3$) than predicted[+]
- Speed drops in outer regions of young spirals - suggests young galaxy interiors more heavily dominated by gas \& stars not dark matter[+]
