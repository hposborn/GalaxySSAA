# The Evolutionary Path of Galaxies

- We have some idea now of what galaxies look like in general:[+]
  - Their main **types** (elliptical, spiral, irregular)[+]
  - Their **observable characteristics** (colour, surface brightness, star formation, velocities, etc)[+]
  - The fundamental **connections** between their derived properties (mass-luminosity-velocity, etc)[+]

# The Evolutionary Path of Galaxies

- But we have only really talked about these properties in the **local universe**[+]
- Fundamentally, the further we observe in distance, the further back in **time** we measure[+]
- By measuring changes between the distant/early and the local/current universe we observe **galaxy evolution**[+]

## Hubble's law

- The more distant a galaxy, the more redshifted its light, $v = H_0 d$ - **Hubble's law**[+]
- We frequently distance directly through **redshift z**, where $ z = \frac{\lambda_{\rm obs}-\lambda_{\rm rest}}{\lambda_{\rm rest}} \sim \frac{H_0 r}{c}$[+]
- The age of a galaxy at a given redshift can be given by: $$t(z) \sim \frac{t_H}{1 + z}$$ where $t_H$ is the Hubble time ($H_0^{-1} \sim 14$Gyr).[+]
 - NB these formula assumes a _constant expansion_... which is only correct for the local universe![+]

## The early universe

- **A denser environment** ($\rho_m \propto (1+z)^3$):[+]
  - Universe had $\sim 1000\times$ matter density $\rho_m$ at $z \sim 9$ than today. [+]
  - The result is that the density (or _close-pair frequency_) of galaxies is much higher[+]
  - Shorter **Dynamical Timescales** ($\tau_{\text{dyn}} \propto H(z)^{-1}$): Higher mass density drives shorter free-fall & orbital timescales. [+]
- **Gas Dominance** ($f_{\text{gas}}$): High-$z$ systems were gas-rich ($f_{\text{gas}} \ge 50\text{--}80$\%), compared to modern stellar-dominated disks and dusty nebulae ($f_{\text{gas}} \lesssim 10\text{--}20$%). [+]

## Morphological Evolution with time

[i=Lenticulars_up_to_z1.png]

- The Hubble Sequence Epoch (z≲1.5): [+]
  - Well-defined disk and elliptical sequences dominate the local universe (z<1). [+]
  - Lenticular (S0) fraction drops from ∼50% in local clusters to <10% at z>1. [+]

-v-

## Morphological Evolution with time

[i=fraction_spheroids_disc_peculiars.gif]

- The Clumpy Disk & Irregular Era (z∼1.5→3): [+]
  - Peculiar/Irregular morphologies dominate (>40% of population). [+]
  - Disks are clumpy and turbulent, dominated by giant star-forming complexes ($M_{\rm clump} \sim10^8 – 10^9 M_\odot$). [+]

-v-

## Morphological Evolution with time

[i=prolate_galaxies_at_distance.jpg]

- The JWST Frontier (z∼3→8+): [+]
  - Prolate/Elongated Geometries: Low-mass galaxies at z>3 are [intrinsically prolate](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/ad1a13/meta) ("cigar/banana" shaped), not flat 2D disks. [+]
  - Early Disks & Bars: JWST reveals regular, rotating disks and stellar bars pushing back to z∼3–5. [+]

-v-

## Sérsic Index (n) Evolution:

[i=Sersic_Index_evolution.png]

- Spheroid profile shapes shift from n≈2 at z∼2 to classic n≈4 (R1/4) profiles locally. [+]
- Driven by continuous central mass accumulation and subsequent dry merger envelope growth. [+]

-v-

## Structural CAS Parameters

[i=CAS_evolution_time.jpg]

- CAS System Evolution across Redshift: [+]
  - **Concentration** (C): High-density central cores (C>3.5) decrease systematically beyond z>1.5. [+]
  - **Asymmetry** (A): Mean asymmetry increases with redshift (A>0.35 at z∼2), driven by high merger rates and clumpy geometries. [+]
  - **Smoothness** (S): Galaxies become increasingly clumpier at high redshift due to internal gravitational instabilities. [+]

## Mass-Size Evolution (z∼0→5)

[i=mass_size_evolution.jpg]

- Mass–Size Evolution ($R_e \propto (1+z)^{-\alpha}$): [+]
  - Radius of distant ellipticals appears far smaller ($R_e$ shrinks with $\alpha \approx 1.2\text{--}1.5$)[+]
  - At fixed mass ($10^{11}M_\odot$), ellipticals at $z\sim2$ are 3–5× smaller than local counterparts. [+]
  - Disk Galaxies show less extreme size growth ($\alpha\approx0.7\text{--}1.0$). [+]

-v-

## The "Red Nugget" Phase (z∼0→5)

[i=NGC_1277_viewed_by_Hubble.jpg]

- Ultra-compact passive spheroids found, especially in $1<z<4$ range. [+]
- Characterized by tiny effective radii ($R_e \sim 1$kpc), high densities ($\rho_e\sim10^{10}M_\odot$ kpc$^{−3}$), and high dispersions ($\sigma\sim300$ km s$^{−1}$). [+]
- Represent the undisturbed, surviving inner cores of modern giant ellipticals. [+]

-v-

## Kinematic Changes (z∼0→4)
[i=Galaxy_kinematic_evolution.png]

- Typically measured in intrinsic velocity dispersions and $V/\sigma$ ratio.[+]

-v-

## Kinematic Changes (z∼0→4)
- Kinematic State Transition ($V/\sigma$ Ratio): [+]
  - At z>2, discs appear turbulent - high intrinsic velocity dispersions ($σ_g \sim 40\text{--}90$ km s$^{−1}$) and low rotational support ($V/\sigma \sim 1\text{--}3$). [+]
  - Disk Settling Phase (z∼1.5→0): Gas dispersion drops ($\sigma_g → 10\text{--}20$ km s$^{−1}$); ordered rotation takes over ($V/\sigma>10$). [+]
- **Kinematic Misalignments** are also frequently observed at $z>1.5$ - gas & stellar components show different velocities due to counter-rotatation. [+]

-v-

## Spatial Clustering & Galaxy Bias (z∼0→6)

[i=Portrait_of_a_galaxy_cluster.jpg]

- *Spatial Clustering Length* ($r_0$): [+]
  - Galaxies are inherently clustered at all distances.[+]
  - $r_0$ refers to a threshold inside of which you are $>2\times$ as likely to find another galaxy compared to pure randomness[+]

## Spatial Clustering & Galaxy Bias (z∼0→6)

[i=R0_evolution_z.png]

- Passive, compact spheroids display larger co-moving correlation lengths ($r_0 \sim 10\text{--}15\text{ h}^{-1}\text{Mpc}$) than low-mass spirals. [+]

## Spatial Clustering & Galaxy Bias (z∼0→6)

[i=Galaxy_bias_evolution_Durkalec.png]

- *Galaxy Bias Evolution*: [+]
  - Defined as the ratio in overdensity rate of galaxies to dark matter: $b(z) \equiv \delta_{\text{gal}} / \delta_{\text{DM}}$[+]
  - Galaxy bias increases strongly with redshift (($b(z) \propto (1+z)^{1.5}$  for massive spheroids). [+]

Note:
- At $z \sim 2\text{--}3$, massive ellipticals inhabit rare, highly dense dark matter halo peaks ($M_{\text{halo}} > 10^{13} M_\odot$). [+]

## Merger terminology
- Gas-rich: **Wet mergers**[+]
  - High cold gas content ($f_{\text{gas}} \gtrsim 20\text{--}80\%$).[+]
  - Hydrodynamic gas shocks drive strong energy dissipation, channeling gas to the galaxy center. [+]
- Gas-poor: **Dry mergers**[+]
  - Low cold gas content ($f_{\text{gas}} \lesssim 10\%$). [+]
  - Dissipationless, collisionless stellar dynamics governed purely by gravitational interactions[+]

-v-

## Building the Core: Gas-Rich "Wet" Mergers (z>1.5)
- Violent Dissipative Collisions: [+]
  - Equal-mass gas-rich major mergers at high redshift drive gas orbital decay into the central potential well. [+]
  - Creates an ultra-dense central stellar core with high Sérsic index ($n \ge 4$). [+]
- Formation of Compact Spheroids: [+]
  - Directly produces the z∼2–5 "red nugget" population in a single rapid collapse event. [+]
- Alternativelty, high gas fractions at z>2 could cause disks to undergo *internal* gravitational collapse, migrating giant clumps inward to build central bulges without major mergers. [+]

-v-

## Expanding the Envelope: Gas-Poor "Dry" Minor Mergers (z<1.5)
- *Satellite Accretion Mechanics*: [+]
  - Low-mass, gas-poor satellite galaxies ($M_1/M_2 < 1/10$) are stripped and destroyed at large radii ($r>R_e$) due to weak tidal binding. [+]
- Deposited stellar material builds extended outer halos without penetrating or heating the central core. [+]
- Dry mergers inflate physical size with minimal mass addition ($R_e \propto M_*^2$), expanding $z \sim 2$ compact red nuggets into giant, extended local ellipticals ($R_e \sim 10\text{--}50\text{ kpc}$). [+]

-v-

## Environmental Structural Transformations (z<1)
- Dense cluster environments can also transform galaxies: [+]
  - **Ram-Pressure Stripping**: Intracluster gas pressure removes interstellar gas from infalling spirals, halting star formation while preserving disk structure. [+]
  - **Strangulation / Starvation**: Removes outer halo gas reservoirs, preventing future disk regrowth. [+]
  - **Galaxy Harassment**: High-speed tidal encounters in dense clusters dynamically heat thin disks into thick, featureless S0s or dEs. [+]
- These explain the structural shift in clusters from blue spirals at z∼1 to passive lenticulars/S0s at z∼0. [+]
- Also creates Dwarf Ellipticals[+]

[P "Take-away messages"]

- *The early universe was a fundamentally different place*
- Galaxies were:
  - Smaller, bluer, clumpier, more turbulent, more prolate, many mergers, etc.
- The "cosmic noon" of star formation, followed by quenching (see next lecture), shaped our current universe.
